How it started

The story of the school, from a twenty-eight-student idea to a public institution the whole town helped build.

1963

Founded as a private school with just twenty-eight students, built on the same principles as Prempeh College — which is where the name "State College" comes from.

Early years

Agogo citizens gave up farmland, and cocoa proceeds were surcharged to fund the new site, as the school outgrew its first home.

1970

Absorbed into Ghana's public school system, becoming Agogo State College as it's known today.

Today

A mixed, day/boarding public SHS offering Business, Home Economics, Visual Arts, General Arts and General Science.

Agogo State College began in 1963 as a privately run school with a handful of students and a founding vision borrowed from Prempeh College — one of Ghana's oldest and most respected secondary schools. The founders wanted the same standard of discipline and academic seriousness here in Agogo.

As demand grew, the school needed more land and more money than a private venture could easily raise. The people of Agogo answered — donating farmland and directing cocoa income toward building the new campus. That community-funded expansion is part of why the school still carries so much local pride.

In 1970, the school was absorbed into Ghana's public education system. It has stayed true to its founding motto ever since: academic excellence, with discipline as its hallmark — for students and teachers alike.
